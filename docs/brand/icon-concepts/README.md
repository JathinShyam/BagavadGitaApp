# Gita Jyoti: app icon concepts

These are six exploration concepts. Each one is a hand-built SVG on a 1024×1024 full-bleed square, with no text and no pre-rounded corners. The key content stays inside the central ~80% (the Android adaptive safe zone). Every concept also has a 1024 px PNG and a 120 px PNG. `contact-sheet.png` shows all six at 300, 120 and 60 px, on light and dark home screens.

Concepts 01, 02, 03 and 05 use the owner's original elements (flute, peacock feather, tilak). Concepts 04 and 06 use the wider Gita and Mahabharata symbols.

**01 · Flute & Feather** (`01-flute-and-feather.svg`): This is the classic Krishna pairing. A gold bansuri with red bindings lies across a peacock feather (mor pankh), on the app's cream-to-gold background. The feather is drawn from tapered barbs with a full eye (gold, green, copper, teal, indigo), so it reads as a feather and not as a leaf. Its strength is that it's instantly recognisable and says exactly what the owner described. Its weakness is that it's the busiest concept: at 60 px the barbs blur into a green smudge and the flute holes disappear. It also looks a lot like the many existing Krishna and Gita apps, so it doesn't stand out.

**02 · Tilak Jyoti** (`02-tilak-jyoti.svg`): Krishna's urdhva-pundra tilak is drawn as a tapered gold U on deep Krishna-blue. The central red line becomes a lamp flame with a pale core. Tilak plus flame says "Gita Jyoti" in a single mark. It uses only 2–3 shapes, so it's the clearest concept at 60 px and works well as a monochrome or notification glyph. The main risk is that the U could be read as a letter "U" or a "V". The tapered, flared arms and the pointed base reduce that risk but don't remove it. The meaning also depends on knowing the urdhva-pundra.

**03 · Mor-pankh Diya** (`03-mor-pankh-diya.svg`): A gold diya on deep maroon. The flame is drawn as the nested rings of a peacock-feather eye (gold, green, teal, indigo), so the feather *is* the jyoti. It combines the owner's main element with the new name in one image. It has a simple silhouette (flame plus bowl) and good colour contrast, and it stays readable at 60 px, where the teal and indigo centre still shows. Its weakness is that a diya is a common Diwali image. The feather-eye flame is what sets it apart, so the colours need to stay accurate in any later version.

**04 · Kurukshetra Dawn** (`04-kurukshetra-dawn.svg`): A 12-spoke chariot wheel rises like the sun over a dark horizon, under a saffron sky. The wheel stands for Krishna's chariot, the Sudarshana chakra, and the dawn on which the Gita was spoken. It's bold, warm and different from the rest of the set, and it holds up well at 60 px. On the downside, it's less clearly "Krishna" or "Gita" than the other concepts. A chariot wheel can also suggest Konark, dharma-chakra imagery or the Indian flag. The dark ground band takes up a quarter of the canvas.

**05 · Lotus & Bansuri, line** (`05-lotus-flute-line.svg`): Gold monoline (30 px stroke) on cream, in the same style as the app's lotus and filigree ornaments. The central lotus petal is a flame with a solid core, and the lotus rests on a flute. This is the best match for the existing UI and the strongest choice for a splash screen or an in-app emblem. As a launcher icon it's quiet: thin gold on cream has low contrast on light wallpapers, and at 60 px the flute holes merge into the outline.

**06 · Panchajanya at Night** (`06-panchajanya-night.svg`): Krishna's conch, whose sound opens the battle in Gita 1.15. It's ivory and gold with a coral aperture, glowing on the app's near-black. It's a fresh choice of symbol with a premium dark look. However, it's the weakest drawing in the set. At small sizes it can read as a generic seashell, or even as food, and it doesn't say "light". Use it only if the conch is a deliberate brand choice, and plan for an illustrator to redraw it.

## Recommendation

1. **03 · Mor-pankh Diya**: This one fuses the owner's peacock feather with the new name's "light" in one bold, legible silhouette. It is the most ownable concept in the set.
2. **02 · Tilak Jyoti**: This is the strongest mark at very small sizes and the most distinctive in the store. It's a good choice if the owner wants a symbol rather than an illustration.

Concept 05 is worth keeping as the splash and in-app emblem, whichever launcher icon is chosen.
